🎯 Diep.io

Diep.io Complete Guide 2026

✍️ By Alex Rivera, Senior Editor 📅 Updated: 2026-07-30 ⏱️ 16 min read
🎮 Play Diep.io Now

Why Diep.io Still Has Me Hooked

I've been writing about browser games for the better part of a decade, and if there's one thing I've learned, it's that most .io games have a shelf life shorter than a gas station sandwich. They explode onto the scene, everyone plays them for two weeks, and then they vanish into the same internet graveyard as Flash games and MySpace.

Diep.io is not one of those games.

It's been running since 2016 — an eternity in browser game years — and it still has a population that fills servers at every hour of the day. I loaded into a Free-For-All match at 3 AM on a Tuesday while writing this guide, and there were 40-some players in the arena. On a Tuesday. At 3 AM.

So what's the deal? Why does this particular tank shooter stick when hundreds of imitators have come and gone?

The answer, I think, comes down to one word: consequence. In Agar.io, you eat dots and get bigger. In Slither.io, you eat dots and get longer. In Diep.io, every single decision you make — which stat to upgrade, which class to evolve into, whether to engage or retreat — has weight. Die, and you lose everything. There's no respawn with your level intact. No second chance. That tension transforms a simple top-down shooter into something genuinely gripping.

The upgrade system is the heart of it. You get 33 stat points across eight categories, and at levels 15, 30, and 45, you choose a tank class from a branching tree containing over 50 options. The combinations are enormous. Two players can pick the same class and have radically different builds, playstyles, and effectiveness. That's depth most browser games don't even attempt.

And the community is still active — the Diep.io subreddit, the Discord servers, the wiki archives, the YouTube content creators. People are still arguing about optimal builds, still theorycrafting new strategies, still posting highlight clips of ridiculous kills. A game that's been around for eight years and still generates that kind of passion is doing something right.

📝 Editor's Note

I came to Diep.io late, around 2020, long after the initial hype. I expected a dead game with empty lobbies. What I found was a community of dedicated players who had spent years refining strategies I'd never even considered. This guide is my attempt to distill what I've learned from them — and from a few hundred hours of my own matches.

Spawn to Level 15: The Survival Gauntlet

You spawn as a circle with a cannon. No name, no upgrades, no identity. Just you, a basic tank, and a map full of shapes and players who want you dead.

The first 15 levels of Diep.io are a survival game. Your job is simple: destroy shapes, gain XP, don't die. But "simple" and "easy" are not the same thing.

Here's what the map gives you to work with:

Squares (yellow) are the bread and butter. They take two hits from a basic tank and give 10 XP each. You'll find them scattered everywhere, but the densest clusters are away from the center.

Triangles (red) give 25 XP and take five hits. Slightly tougher, slightly better. You'll start targeting these once you have a few upgrade points under your belt.

Pentagons (blue) are the prize — 130 XP each, but they take 22 hits from a stock tank. They cluster in the Pentagon Nest at the center of the map, which is also where every high-level player in the server congregates. Going there at level 5 is a death sentence.

My advice for the first 15 levels? Stick to the edges. Farm squares and triangles. Turn on auto-fire by pressing E — it keeps your cannon shooting constantly so you can focus on movement. If you see a high-level tank approaching, run. Don't try to be a hero at level 8.

The level 15 choice is your first real decision, and it shapes everything that follows:

  • Twin — adds a second cannon. Higher fire rate, more recoil. Great if you want to spam bullets.
  • Sniper — wider field of view, faster bullets, slower reload. The pick for players who like range.
  • Machine Gun — trapezoidal barrel that fires rapidly with a spread. Less accurate but covers a wide area.
  • Flank Guard — adds a rear cannon. You can shoot forward and backward simultaneously. Zero net recoil.

Or you can skip all four and wait. Do this at level 15 and again at level 30, and you unlock the Smasher — a spiked ramming tank that trades guns entirely for body damage. It's a bold choice, and a story for later.

33 Points, 8 Stats, Zero Excuses

Every time you level up, you get one stat point. By level 45, you'll have accumulated 33 of them. You distribute these across eight categories, each of which caps at 7. That's the entire build system, and it's more strategically rich than it sounds.

Here are your eight stats, in the order they appear on the upgrade bar:

  1. Health Regen — How fast you recover HP after taking damage. Undervalued by new players, loved by veterans.
  2. Max Health — Your total HP pool. More health means you survive longer in firefights.
  3. Body Damage — Damage dealt when your tank physically collides with something. Essential for ramming builds, useless for most others.
  4. Bullet Speed — How fast your projectiles travel. Faster bullets are harder to dodge and reach targets quicker.
  5. Bullet Penetration — How many objects your bullet passes through before disappearing. This is the stat that separates good players from dead ones.
  6. Bullet Damage — Raw damage per hit. Straightforward.
  7. Reload — Fire rate. More bullets per second equals more damage per second.
  8. Movement Speed — How fast your tank moves. Critical for escaping, chasing, and dodging.

Now here's where it gets interesting. You can't max everything. 33 points across 8 stats that cap at 7 means you'll max out four stats (28 points) and have 5 left for the rest. Every point you put into Health is a point you can't put into Bullet Damage. Every point in Movement Speed is a point not in Reload. Every build is a series of trade-offs.

The community has names for different build philosophies. The most famous is the Glass Cannon — zero health, zero regen, zero body damage, everything into bullets and movement. You hit like a freight train and die if someone sneezes on you. It's terrifying to play and terrifying to fight.

At the other extreme is the Rammer — everything into health, body damage, and movement speed. No bullets worth mentioning. You kill people by running into them at full speed. It sounds dumb until a Booster with 7/7/7 in health/damage/speed comes flying out of nowhere and one-shots you.

💡 Pro Insight

The single most common mistake I see new players make is spreading points evenly across all eight stats. A tank with 4 points in everything is bad at everything. Pick 3-4 stats that matter for your build and push them to 7. Specialization wins in Diep.io. Always.

The Class Tree: More Branches Than a Banyan

Hold the Y key in-game and the class tree appears — a set of concentric rings showing every tank in the game, with your current tank in the center. It's one of the most satisfying upgrade trees in any browser game, and it's also one of the most overwhelming.

Let me break it down by tier.

Tier 2 (Level 15): Four choices — Twin, Sniper, Machine Gun, Flank Guard. Or skip to unlock Smasher later. I covered these in the previous section, so I won't repeat myself. What matters is that this choice determines which branches are available to you at levels 30 and 45.

Tier 3 (Level 30): This is where the tree explodes. From the Twin line, you get Triple Shot, Quad Tank, and Twin Flank. From Sniper: Assassin and Overseer. From Machine Gun: Destroyer and Gunner. From Flank Guard: Quad Tank (shared with Twin), Auto 3, and Tri-Angle. Each of these plays fundamentally differently. The Overseer, for instance, replaces your cannons with drone spawners — you control a swarm of AI minions instead of shooting bullets. It's a completely different game.

Tier 4 (Level 45): The final form. This is where you get the tanks that people actually talk about:

  • Overlord (from Overseer) — Four drone spawners. The highest skill ceiling in the game. Good players are untouchable; bad players are free kills.
  • Penta Shot (from Triple Shot) — Five forward cannons. A wall of bullets that shreds anything in front of you.
  • Annihilator (from Destroyer) — One massive cannon that fires shells the size of a pentagon. If it hits, you're dead. If it misses, you're vulnerable for two seconds.
  • Fighter (from Tri-Angle) — A speed demon with guns pointing in every direction. The most mobile tank in the game.
  • Factory (from Overseer, alternate path) — Spawns mini-tanks that auto-fire. Like having your own personal army.
  • Necromancer (from Sniper, alternate path) — Converts destroyed squares into drones. The more you farm, the stronger your swarm.

And that's maybe a third of the level 45 roster. There are over 50 tanks in total, each with its own playstyle, counters, and optimal build.

The community meta shifts depending on who you ask. Over on the Diep.io subreddit, the consensus for FFA tends to favor Overlord, Penta Shot, and Fighter — tanks that can both farm efficiently and defend themselves. In team modes, Necromancer and Tri-Trapper shine because their utility scales with coordination. In Maze, tanks that can control chokepoints — Spread Shot, Octo Tank — become dominant.

There's no "best" tank. There's only the best tank for the situation you're in, the mode you're playing, and the build you're running. And that's what makes the class tree worth caring about.

Four Builds the Community Swears By

Let's get specific. Here are four builds that have been battle-tested by the community, with exact stat allocations. Stats are listed in order: Health Regen / Max Health / Body Damage / Bullet Speed / Bullet Penetration / Bullet Damage / Reload / Movement Speed.

1. Glass Cannon Penta Shot — 0/0/0/7/7/7/7/5

No health. No regen. No body damage. Every point into making your bullets as lethal as possible, with just enough movement speed to dodge. The Penta Shot fires five streams of bullets, and with maxed penetration and damage, each stream chews through everything in its path. You can farm the Pentagon Nest solo. You can win 1v1 fights against almost anything. You can also die in two seconds if a Booster rams you.

This is the build that most experienced players gravitate toward in FFA. It's high-risk, high-reward, and it teaches you positioning faster than any other build because mistakes are instantly fatal.

2. Overlord Drone Controller — 0/2/0/7/7/7/5/5

The Overlord's drones inherit your bullet stats, so maxing penetration and damage is non-negotiable. The two points in Max Health give you just enough survivability to survive a hit you didn't see coming. Reload controls how fast your drones respawn, which is critical — an Overlord without drones is a sitting duck.

Playing Overlord well requires constant multitasking. You're managing your drone swarm, watching for incoming fire, repositioning, and looking for ambush opportunities — all at once. A player I met on the Diep.io Discord described it as "playing chess while someone is shooting at you." That about covers it.

3. Ramming Booster — 7/7/7/0/2/0/3/7

This is the build that makes people rage. The Booster has rear thrusters that make it the fastest tank in the game. With max health, body damage, and movement speed, you become a guided missile. You fly across the map at full speed and crash into people. If they're not built for survivability, they die instantly.

The two points in Bullet Penetration aren't for damage — they're so your bullets can knock back incoming fire slightly, giving you a clearer approach. It's a niche build and a controversial one. Half the community thinks it's cheesy; the other half respects the commitment.

4. Hybrid Bruiser — 2/3/0/7/7/7/7/0

The Hybrid is a Destroyer-class tank with two Overseer drones on the back. You get a massive front cannon for burst damage and two AI drones for defense. This build balances health and firepower — enough survivability to take a hit, enough damage to win trades. Zero movement speed means you're slow, but with maxed reload and bullet speed, your cannon fires fast enough to create recoil that pushes you around the map.

A Steam community player reported hitting 200K points with this build in 4 Teams. I've personally used it to hold the #1 spot in FFA for an entire arena cycle. It's consistent, forgiving, and genuinely fun.

The Pentagon Nest: Where Scores Are Made and Broken

The center of every Diep.io map is the Pentagon Nest — a spawning ground for high-value pentagons and the occasional Alpha Pentagon, a massive shape worth thousands of XP. It's also the most dangerous spot on the map, because every high-level player in the server gravitates there.

Farming the Nest is the fastest way to level up once you're past level 30. A single Alpha Pentagon gives 3,000 XP — enough to jump several levels in one kill. But the competition is fierce, and the Nest is a wide-open area with no cover. If a Penta Shot catches you in the open, you're done.

My strategy for Nest farming: go in with a build that can deal with crowds. Penta Shot and Octo Tank excel here because they can shoot in multiple directions while farming. Drone classes like Overlord and Necromancer are also strong — send your swarm to collect pentagons while you hang back at a safe distance.

Bosses add another layer of chaos. Every 10-23 minutes, a boss spawns in or near the Nest. There are five types:

  • Guardian — A crasher-shaped boss that spawns smaller crashers
  • Summoner — A square-shaped boss that spawns necromancer-style drones
  • Defender — A triangle boss with auto-turrets
  • Fallen Booster — A giant Booster that rams players
  • Fallen Overlord — A massive Overlord with enormous drone swarms

Killing a boss grants 30,000 XP — enough to take a level 1 tank straight to 45. The entire server converges when a boss spawns, and the resulting chaos is some of the most fun you can have in Diep.io. My advice: if you're below level 30, stay away. Bosses will one-shot you without noticing. If you're level 45 with a good build, get in there and fight for the kill — but watch your back, because half the server is using the boss fight as cover to pick off distracted players.

Player vs Player: The Mind Games Begin

Farming shapes is the tutorial. Fighting other players is the game.

Diep.io PvP is psychological in ways that aren't immediately obvious. Your opponent's tank class is visible — it tells you their capabilities, their likely build, and their threat level. An Annihilator at close range is a death sentence. An Annihilator at long range is a free kill. Reading these signals is the difference between climbing the leaderboard and respawning at level 1.

Let me walk you through a real match scenario. I'm playing Overlord in FFA, sitting at level 45 with the build I described earlier. I'm farming pentagons in the Nest when I see a Booster approaching from the right at high speed. Booster plus high speed means rammer. I know instantly that this player has maxed body damage and movement speed. If they touch me, I'm dead — my build has zero health investment.

My drones are out farming on the left side. I can't recall them in time. So I do the only thing I can: I turn and run, firing bullets behind me to create recoil that boosts my speed. The Booster is faster, but my bullets slow them slightly. I manage to survive long enough to recall my drones and form a defensive screen. The Booster sees the drones, recognizes the trap, and disengages.

That exchange took maybe four seconds. But in those four seconds, I had to identify the threat, assess my options, execute a retreat, and set up a counter. That's what Diep.io PvP looks like at a decent level.

💡 Pro Insight

The most important PvP skill is knowing when not to fight. If you're at half health, if your drones are on cooldown, if you're facing two opponents — retreat. There's no penalty for running except your pride, and pride doesn't count for anything when you're respawning at level 1. The players who maintain long leaderboard streaks aren't the ones who win every fight. They're the ones who pick their fights.

Some general matchup knowledge that'll save your life: drone classes (Overlord, Necromancer, Manager) struggle against bullet spammers (Penta Shot, Spread Shot, Octo Tank) because the bullet wall shreds drones before they can connect. Bullet spammers, in turn, are vulnerable to snipers (Ranger, Stalker, Predator) who can hit them from outside their range. And everyone is vulnerable to a rammer they didn't see coming. It's not quite rock-paper-scissors, but the counter relationships are real and worth learning.

Diep.io vs Arras.io vs Tankr.io: The Sibling Rivalry

If you've spent any time in the Diep.io community, you've heard of Arras.io. It's the elephant in the room — a fan-made successor that takes the Diep.io formula and cranks it to eleven. And then there's Tankr.io, a simpler take on the tank shooter concept. Let me break down how they compare.

Arras.io is, for my money, the most ambitious .io game mod ever made. It takes Diep.io's class tree — which has 50+ tanks — and expands it to over 500. There are tanks that lay mines, tanks that summon minions, tanks that teleport, tanks with shields, tanks that heal. The dev team regularly adds new content. There are boss raids, custom game modes, and a competitive scene that's separate from Diep.io's.

The trade-off? Complexity. Arras.io has a steeper learning curve than Diep.io, and the sheer number of tanks means balance is an ongoing concern. Some classes are clearly better than others, and the meta shifts with every update. If you've mastered Diep.io and want more, Arras.io is the natural next step. If you're new to the genre, it might be overwhelming.

Diep.io itself is the Goldilocks option. It's deep enough to reward investment but simple enough that a new player can understand the basics in five minutes. The class tree is large but manageable. The builds are varied but not infinite. The community is established and the meta is relatively stable. It's the game I'd recommend to anyone who hasn't played a tank .io game before.

Tankr.io is the lightweight alternative. It has a smaller class tree, simpler mechanics, and a more casual feel. Think of it as Diep.io Lite — good for quick sessions when you don't want to commit to a full 20-minute match. The player base is smaller, which means longer queue times and less variety in opponents. But the core gameplay loop is satisfying, and the lower complexity makes it accessible.

Here's my take: start with Diep.io. Play it until you've tried at least five different level 45 classes and found one that clicks. If you hit a wall and want more depth — more tanks, more builds, more chaos — move to Arras.io. Tankr.io is worth checking out if you want a more relaxed experience, but it's not a replacement for either of the others.

One thing worth mentioning: Diep.io and Arras.io share a community. Players move between them freely, and strategies often cross-pollinate. A build that works in Diep.io usually has an Arras.io equivalent, and vice versa. The games are different enough to feel distinct but similar enough that skills transfer.

Six Modes, Six Completely Different Games

Diep.io has seven game modes, and they might as well be seven different games. Each one rewards different builds, different playstyles, and different levels of aggression. Here's the breakdown:

Free-For-All (FFA): The classic. Every tank for themselves. No teams, no respawns, no mercy. The leaderboard updates in real time, and the goal is simple: survive as long as possible and rack up the highest score. FFA favors self-sufficient builds — Penta Shot, Overlord, Fighter. Anything that can fight 1v1 and escape 1v2.

2 Teams: Red vs. Blue. You share a base with your team, and base drones protect your spawn area. Team modes are more forgiving for new players because teammates can protect you while you level up. They're also the best mode for support tanks — Tri-Trapper, for instance, is nearly useless in FFA but invaluable in team modes where its wall of traps can shield allies.

4 Teams: Same concept, more chaos. Four factions competing simultaneously means the map is more crowded and the action is more constant. This is the mode I recommend for new players — it's easier to reach level 45 because you have teammates covering your back.

Domination: Two teams fight to capture giant AI-controlled turrets called Dominators. Once a team captures all the Dominators, they win. It's objective-based, which means kills matter less than positioning. Tanks that can hold territory — Trapper classes, Octo Tank — shine here.

Maze: The map is divided by walls, creating corridors and chokepoints. This fundamentally changes the game. Sniper classes become terrifying because they can shoot down hallways. Ramming classes become useless because there's no room to build speed. Maze is the most tactical mode in Diep.io, and it's where I've had some of my most memorable matches.

Tag: Two teams. When you kill an enemy, they join your team. First team to absorb everyone wins. It's fast, chaotic, and often decided in the first two minutes. Snowballing is real — once one team gets an advantage, it compounds quickly.

Sandbox: The training mode. You're alone (or with friends) and have access to developer commands. Press K to instantly reach level 45, backslash to cycle through every tank, O to self-destruct. Use Sandbox to test builds before taking them into real matches.

Things I Wish Someone Had Told Me at Level 1

I'm going to save you some time. Here are the lessons I learned the hard way — by dying repeatedly and questioning my life choices.

Bullet Penetration is the most underrated stat. New players dump points into Bullet Damage and Reload because those feel impactful. But Penetration — the ability of your bullets to pass through shapes and enemy fire — is what keeps you alive. A bullet with high penetration can block incoming shots, effectively creating a shield. In a firefight, the player with higher penetration wins the bullet-vs-bullet exchange. I didn't understand this for my first 50 hours of play.

Auto-fire (E) and Auto-spin (C) are not just for lazy people. Auto-fire keeps your cannon shooting continuously, which is essential for farming and for maintaining bullet pressure in fights. Auto-spin rotates your cannon automatically, which sounds useless until you play a Flank Guard or Octo Tank and realize you can focus entirely on movement while your guns do the aiming.

Your minimap is more important than your screen. The minimap shows the location of every player on the map. Check it constantly. If you see a cluster of dots converging on your position, it's time to move. If you see a lone dot approaching fast, it's probably a Booster coming to ram you.

The Pentagon Nest is not your friend at level 15. I don't care how tempting those 130-XP pentagons look. At level 15, you're food for every level 45 in the server. Farm the edges until you're at least level 30, then cautiously approach the Nest with a build that can defend itself.

Health Regen is a valid build choice. The meta says glass cannon or nothing, but 7 points in Health Regen means you can take a hit, retreat for ten seconds, and come back at full health. In long FFA sessions, that sustained presence matters more than raw burst damage.

📝 Editor's Note

If there's one piece of advice I'd tattoo on my forehead, it's this: use Sandbox mode. Test your build before you commit to it in a real match. The number of times I've hit level 45, allocated my stats, and immediately realized I'd made a terrible mistake is embarrassing. Sandbox takes 30 seconds and saves you 20 minutes of regret.

Questions That Won't Stop Coming Up

These are the questions I see posted in the Diep.io Discord and subreddit at least once a week. Here are the answers.

What's the best tank for beginners?

The Twin line. Pick Twin at level 15, then Triple Shot at level 30, then Penta Shot or Triple Twin at level 45. The Twin line teaches you fundamentals — bullet timing, positioning, recoil management — without requiring the multitasking of drone classes or the precision of snipers. Once you're comfortable, branch out.

How do I level up faster?

Farm the Pentagon Nest once you're level 30+. Pentagons give 130 XP each, and Alpha Pentagons give 3,000. If a boss spawns, get to it — a boss kill is worth 30,000 XP. In team modes, stick with your teammates and let them cover you while you farm.

What should I upgrade first?

For bullet builds: Bullet Penetration and Bullet Damage first, then Reload and Bullet Speed. For ramming builds: Body Damage and Movement Speed first, then Max Health. Health Regen is a good secondary investment for any build.

How do I counter an Overlord?

Overlords are powerful but have a critical weakness: they're defenseless when their drones are far away. Bullet spammers can overwhelm the drones with volume of fire. Snipers can pick off the Overlord from outside its range. And a fast rammer can reach the Overlord before its drones can recall. The key is aggression — don't let the Overlord set the pace of the fight.

What's the maximum level?

45. You reach it by accumulating XP from shapes and player kills. Once you're level 45, you stop gaining levels but continue accumulating score, which determines your leaderboard position.

Closing Thoughts from the Editor's Desk

Diep.io is the kind of game that shouldn't work. It's a browser-based tank shooter with simple graphics, a single map layout, and a community that's been playing for nearly a decade. By all rights, it should be dead.

It's not. And after spending more time than I care to admit in its arenas, I understand why. The build system creates depth. The permadeath creates tension. The class tree creates variety. And the community creates the kind of meta-discussion that keeps a game alive long after its novelty wears off.

If you're new, start with 4 Teams. Pick Twin. Farm shapes. Don't worry about the leaderboard. Learn the stats, learn the classes, learn the maps. Die a lot. It's fine — everyone does.

Then, when you're ready, go to FFA. Pick a build from this guide. Hold the Y key and stare at that class tree until you find the tank that speaks to you. And when you finally hit level 45, when your build comes online and you feel that power spike — that's when Diep.io clicks. That's when it stops being a browser game and becomes something you think about at work.

I'll see you in the Pentagon Nest.
