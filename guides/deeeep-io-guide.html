Deeeep.io: Every Apex Predator Builds Ranked — Which One Actually Wins?

The Big Question

Everyone argues about which evolution path is the absolute best in Deeeep.io. Do you turtle up in the shallow rivers, or do you dive into the crushing depths to become a leviathan? I’ve spent hundreds of hours testing every biome, managing oxygen meters, and getting ambushed by Bobbit Worms so you don’t have to. The truth is, your playstyle completely changes depending on whether you choose freshwater tanks, pelagic speedsters, or deep-sea brawlers. Today, I’m ranking the top four Apex Predator builds from worst to best. I’m bringing hard data on oxygen drain, depth tiers, and damage output. Buckle up, because my number one pick might just ruin your shallow-water meta.

The Contenders

Cachalot — The Deep Sea Brawler

Operates in the Abyssal zone (4000m+ depth), massive health pool, high bite damage, but suffers a severe 2x oxygen drain rate when surfacing to breathe.
✓ Unmatched in deep-water skirmishes. Its sheer health pool allows it to absorb hits from multiple mid-tier predators. The bite damage melts through the armor of crabs and turtles. It dominates the oxygen-rich vents and controls the deep-sea food chain. You can just sit in the trenches and force smaller fish to come to you. The pressure tolerance means you never have to worry about the environmental hazards that kill off lesser builds. It is the ultimate zone controller.
✗ Extremely vulnerable to oxygen starvation. If you get chased to the surface by pelagic hunters, you suffocate quickly due to the massive oxygen drain. The slow turning radius makes it incredibly easy to kite by faster creatures like Orcas. You also struggle against coordinated pack hunters who can bait you out of your safe depth zones. If you misjudge your oxygen bar by even a few seconds, you die to the environment instead of a player.
The undisputed king of the deep, but a sitting duck in the shallows.

Hippo — The Shallow Tank

Dominates the Freshwater and Shallow zones (0-500m), incredible sprint speed on land, massive HP, but restricted to surface-level combat and zero pressure resistance.
✓ The ultimate shallow-water bully. Its unique ability to transition between water and land gives it unparalleled map control in rivers and coastlines. You can ambush prey on the beach and retreat to the water, completely bypassing the aquatic-only limitations of other tanks. The sprint speed on land is surprisingly high, allowing you to catch fleeing freshwater fish or escape aquatic predators that can't follow you onto the mud. It completely owns the 0-500m depth range.
✗ Completely useless in the deep ocean. If a Cachalot or Whale pushes into the shallows, you get out-scaled instantly. Also, highly susceptible to poison and bleed effects from aquatic ambushers because you can't easily retreat to deep vents to heal. The total lack of pressure resistance means you can't even dive below 500m to escape. Once the mid-game hits and players evolve into deep-sea monsters, the Hippo just becomes a walking snack for the trenches.
A shallow-water tyrant that completely falls off once you hit the drop-off.

Orca — The Pelagic Speedster

Cruises in the Epipelagic zone (0-1000m), top-tier swim speed multiplier, high stamina regeneration, but low base health compared to true deep-sea tanks.
✓ Hit-and-run perfection. The Orca's top-tier speed allows it to dictate every single engagement. You can chase down fleeing fish, escape deep-sea monsters, and control the oxygen economy by easily darting to the surface. It thrives in team play, coordinating with other Orcas to isolate and drown larger targets. The high stamina regeneration means you can sustain long chases without gassing out. You are the absolute apex of the Epipelagic zone, completely untouchable by anything slower.
✗ Glass cannon fragility. One bad mistake against a Bobbit Worm or a coordinated Crab defense, and you're dead instantly. It lacks the raw bite damage to one-shot heavy tanks, requiring prolonged chipping that heavily risks oxygen depletion. If you get cornered in a tight cave or trapped by a Mantis Shrimp, your speed advantage completely vanishes. You are always one bad hit away from dying. It demands constant map awareness and punishes every single positioning error with immediate death.
The ultimate hit-and-run assassin, but punishes every single positioning error.

Anglerfish — The Abyssal Ambusher

Lurks in the Bathyal and Abyssal zones (1000m-4000m), features a unique bioluminescent lure mechanic, moderate health, but devastating burst damage in close quarters.
✓ The bioluminescent lure mechanic is a game-changer for baiting greedy predators. You can sit completely still, conserve oxygen, and wait for mid-tier fish to investigate the light. When they get close, the burst damage deletes them instantly. It completely controls the dark zones where vision is limited. The psychological warfare of watching a smaller fish swim right into your jaws because they couldn't resist the glowing lure is incredibly satisfying. You truly own the shadows.
✗ If your ambush fails, you're in massive trouble. The Anglerfish lacks the chase speed to catch evasive targets. Against high-mobility predators like the Manta Ray, your lure is completely useless, and you get kited to death while your oxygen drains. You also have moderate health, meaning if a larger tank catches you off guard, you don't have the bulk to survive the initial hit. It's a high-risk, high-reward build that requires extreme patience.
A brilliant trap-setter that becomes completely helpless in a straight footrace.

The Rankings

#1 Cachalot Rank 1 of 4
#2 Hippo Rank 2 of 4
#3 Orca Rank 3 of 4
#4 Anglerfish Rank 4 of 4

Let’s get right into the definitive ranking, from worst to best. Starting at number four, the Anglerfish. It’s a brilliant trap-setter, but it’s completely helpless in a straight footrace. If your ambush fails, you just get kited to death by anything with basic mobility. It’s too reliant on enemy mistakes. At number three, the Hippo. It’s an absolute tyrant in the early game and controls the coastlines perfectly. However, it’s hard-capped by the shallow zone. Once you hit the drop-off, you completely fall off against deep-sea monsters. Number two is the Orca. The hit-and-run perfection is unmatched, and the map control is incredible. But it requires intense team coordination to take down true tanks, and one positioning error means death. Finally, number one: the Cachalot. The undisputed apex. If you master the oxygen management, nothing beats its raw stats and zone control. It forces the enemy to play on your terms in the deep trenches.

Who Should Pick What

If you're a beginner
Go Hippo
Shallow waters are incredibly forgiving for new players. The unique land-water transition teaches you essential map awareness and escape routes without the brutal oxygen penalties and pressure mechanics of the deep sea.
If you love aggression
Go Orca
You get to dictate the pace of the game entirely. Constantly hunting, kiting, and harassing opponents while staying safely near the surface gives you the ultimate aggressive playmaker experience.
If you play defensively
Go Cachalot
The massive health pool allows you to easily survive early-game mistakes. Dominating the deep sea trenches means you face fewer unpredictable ganks from surface players, letting you farm safely.
If you love outsmarting opponents
Go Anglerfish
Using the bioluminescent lure to bait greedy players into your jaws provides the most satisfying psychological plays. It rewards patience and punishes opponents who lack the discipline to ignore the light.

The Bottom Line

The Cachalot takes the crown as the ultimate apex build. While Orcas are flashy and Hippos dominate the early game, the Cachalot's sheer survivability and deep-sea dominance make it the undisputed endgame king. Mastering the oxygen management between the abyss and the surface is the highest skill ceiling in Deeeep.io. If you want to truly rule the ocean, stop playing in the kiddie pool and take the Cachalot to the trenches. Trust me, once you feel the power of a full-health Sperm Whale, you'll never go back to the shallows.
