1

You Overextend After Becoming King

What You’re Doing Wrong

You finally hit the 20% threshold and become the King. Then you make the stupidest mistake possible. You leave your safe zone to grab an extra five percent. You sprint across the open map with a massive tail, thinking your speed bonus makes you invincible. You act like a greedy target instead of a tactical player, ignoring the fact that you are now the most hunted person in the entire lobby. You completely forget that the game just changed.

Why It’s Fatal

Becoming the King means your exact position is broadcasted on the minimap to all 45 players. Your giant tail is now a target for every sniper in the lobby. The speed bonus will not save you when you collide with an enemy head outside your territory or get your tail sliced. You literally hand the victory to your opponents by dying before the three-minute countdown ends, throwing away a guaranteed win.

How to Fix It

The second you become the King, stop expanding immediately. Turtle inside your 20% territory. Use your speed bonus to patrol your borders and slice the tails of enemies creeping close to your edge. Your only objective is surviving the three-minute timer. Do not chase perfect map coverage. Stay inside, stay safe, and let the timer hit zero to secure your absolute victory.

2

You Charge Into Slowing Clouds

What You’re Doing Wrong

You spot an enemy running away near a cloudy area. You immediately chase them straight into the slowing cloud generated by a slowing totem. You think it is just a visual effect or that your raw speed can overpower it. You ignore the trap completely and push deep into enemy territory without checking the totems, walking right into a fatal ambush that you could have easily avoided.

Why It’s Fatal

That cloud drastically reduces your movement speed. Once you are inside, you cannot turn quickly or escape. The enemy simply stops running, turns around, and slices your tail while you are moving in slow motion. Alternatively, they just walk their head into yours. In a slowdown zone, you lack the reaction time to dodge. You die instantly and pointlessly, giving away your hard-earned territory.

How to Fix It

Treat every slowing cloud like a lethal minefield. Never enter it unless you already own that specific hexagonal territory. Route your movement around the cloudy areas. If you need to clear it, use a spreading totem to capture the hexes from a safe distance. If you get caught inside, use a teleport gate or PRO item to escape immediately. Never fight inside a zone that cripples your mobility.

3

You Draw Massive Loops in the Open

What You’re Doing Wrong

You want to capture a huge chunk of the map in one single move. You leave your territory and draw a massive circle across the center of the map. Your tail stretches across half the arena. You believe that if you just run fast enough, nobody can reach your incredibly long tail to slice it. This arrogant mindset is the primary reason you keep dying in the open.

Why It’s Fatal

In this 3D hexagonal grid, a long tail means massive exposure. Enemies do not even need to chase you. They simply wait in the middle of your loop and slice your tail in half, or they park their head directly on your return path. The bigger the circle you draw, the easier you make it for them to eliminate you. You are basically doing their job for them.

How to Fix It

Only draw small, tight loops that you can complete in a few seconds. Expand your territory by two or three rows of hexagons at a time. A short tail gives enemies zero space to slice it. Play patiently. Accumulate your territory through dozens of safe, micro-captures rather than risking your entire game on one greedy, map-spanning maneuver. Consistency beats risky plays every single time.

4

You Abuse the Teleport Gate

What You’re Doing Wrong

You pick up the teleport gate totem. It teleports you to the furthest point of your territory. To save time running back, you activate it while your tail is still dragging outside your borders. You think it is a free fast-travel mechanic to skip the boring walk back to your safe zone. You fundamentally misunderstand how the game calculates your territory footprint during transit.

Why It’s Fatal

The teleport gate calculates your current owned territory. If you activate it mid-loop, your territory footprint changes instantly, and you might teleport into a dead end surrounded by enemies. Worse, if the furthest point of your territory is currently being contested, you will teleport directly into an enemy trap or collide with their head upon arrival. It is an instant self-elimination.

How to Fix It

Only use the teleport gate after you have successfully connected your tail back to your main territory. Before clicking it, check your minimap to ensure the furthest point of your land is completely clear of enemies. Use it strictly for emergency escapes or strategic repositioning, never as a casual shortcut while your tail is still exposed. Patience prevents these stupid, avoidable deaths.

5

You Steal Totems from Your Duo Partner

What You’re Doing Wrong

You are playing Duo mode where you share territory. You see a speed totem spawn and immediately rush to grab it, completely ignoring that your partner already picked up the spreading totem. You treat a cooperative team mode like a selfish free-for-all, hoarding offensive buffs while your backline is completely undefended. You prioritize your personal stats over the team's actual survival.

Why It’s Fatal

Duo mode requires map control and shared defense. If both of you grab aggressive totems, nobody is watching your shared borders. Enemies will easily flank your massive combined territory and slice both of your tails simultaneously. You lose the match because you prioritized your personal speed buff over your team's structural integrity and survival. Your selfishness directly causes a double elimination.

How to Fix It

Communicate with your partner before the match starts. Assign roles. One player takes the speed or spreading totem to push the frontline, while the other grabs the slowing totem or spy dish to defend the rear. If your partner takes an offensive totem, you must pick up a defensive one. Synergy wins Duo matches, not individual greed. Play your role.

The Bottom Line

The biggest difference between a Copper noob and an Elite veteran in Hexanaut.io is pure patience. Amateurs chase the 20% King title or draw massive loops to get a quick dopamine hit. Veterans play the map, respect the totems, and keep their tails short. Stop playing for the highlight reel and start playing for the three-minute countdown. Fix your positioning, control your greed, and go claim your crown.
