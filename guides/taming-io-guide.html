The Big Question

Everyone argues about which starting pet is the absolute best in Taming.io, so I locked myself in and tested every early-game strategy for 48 hours straight. The community is deeply divided between the fire-spamming Wolf, the tanky Bear, and the speedy Rabbit. But here is the truth nobody wants to admit: picking a pet is only half the battle. Your actual playstyle dictates whether you survive past Age 10 or get farmed for XP. I’ve crunched the data, analyzed the matchup spreads, and ranked the top five early-game strategies from absolute trash to undisputed meta. If you want to stop dying in the first five minutes and actually dominate the server, you need to see this final ranking. Let’s dive in.

The Contenders

Wolf Aggro — The Fireball Spammer

Fire-type pet with a fireball skill that deals constant AoE damage to nearby enemies. Standard base speed and regeneration stats.
✓ The Wolf is the undisputed king of the first ten Ages. Its fireball skill completely melts wild animals, making XP farming incredibly efficient. In player matchups, the constant AoE damage creates a deadly zone around you that completely shuts down melee players who try to rush you. It forces opponents to respect your space, allowing you to control early-game resource nodes and chokepoints. When you pair this with early spike traps, you create an inescapable kill box that guarantees easy kills and massive XP leads.
✗ The fatal flaw of the Wolf strategy is its massive drop-off in the mid-game. Once players unlock ranged weapons around Age 15, your fireball becomes practically useless. Ranged players will simply kite you, staying just outside your AoE range while peppering you with arrows. You also lack the raw survivability to face-tank this damage, meaning if you get caught out of position by a coordinated team, you will melt before you can even close the gap to use your skill.
A solid early-game bully that gets completely outclassed the second your opponent unlocks a bow.

Bear Tank — The Melee Bruiser

Highest base HP and regeneration of the starting trio. Synergizes heavily with early melee weapons and structure spam like walls and spikes.
✓ If you want to feel invincible in the early game, the Bear is your best friend. The massive HP pool and passive regeneration allow you to face-tank wild animals and brawl other early players without needing to retreat for food. You can aggressively push into enemy territory, placing walls and doors to trap slower opponents. In a pure 1v1 melee scenario before ranged weapons unlock, the Bear simply outlasts the Wolf and Rabbit, grinding them down through sheer attrition and raw, unadulterated stats.
✗ Let’s be brutally honest: the Bear is a massive beginner trap. Your movement speed is terribly slow, which means you will never catch a Rabbit player who decides to run away. Worse, you are incredibly easy to kite. Any competent player with a ranged weapon will just backpedal and shoot you to death while you slowly waddle toward them. You have zero tools to close the gap, making you completely helpless against hit-and-run tactics and completely reliant on your enemies making a mistake.
The ultimate beginner trap that feels like a god until you realize you literally cannot hit anything.

Rabbit Kite — The Mobility Master

Highest base speed of the starting pets. Lowest base HP, relying entirely on hit-and-run mechanics and positioning to survive.
✓ Mobility is the true currency in Taming.io, and the Rabbit prints money. Your unmatched speed gives you total map control, allowing you to grab resources, hit ore for XP, and vanish before a Bear can even turn around. You dictate every engagement. If a fight goes poorly, you simply run away. This playstyle is perfect for playing defensively, hoarding resources, and picking off stragglers. Because you are constantly moving, you are incredibly hard to hit with skill shots, making you a nightmare for slower, clunky players to deal with in the early and mid-game.
✗ The Rabbit has absolutely zero staying power. If you get cornered against a wall or caught in a tight space by a Wolf’s AoE fireball, you melt instantly. You cannot afford to make a single positioning mistake. Furthermore, your low HP means you cannot contest heavily fortified bases or push through chokepoints without taking massive damage. You are entirely reliant on your ability to escape, meaning if the map gets too crowded or you get ganked by a team, your speed won't save you from being surrounded and deleted.
The only starting pet that actually scales perfectly into the mid-game if you have the mechanical skill to play it.

Base Turtle — The Structure Spammer

Focuses purely on Age progression and structure spam. Ignores pet combat stats in favor of walls, doors, and turrets.
✓ This strategy is all about patience and infrastructure. By ignoring early-game pet fights and focusing purely on hitting ore to reach Age 15, you can build a heavily fortified compound. Once your walls and turrets are up, you have a completely safe zone to farm XP and hoard resources while other players die in the wild. It is an incredibly frustrating strategy to play against, as it forces aggressive players to waste their resources trying to break through your defenses, often leading them to rage quit out of pure boredom and frustration.
✗ It is a fundamentally cowardly and boring way to play. You are completely helpless in the open world, meaning if you step outside your walls without your pet, you are a sitting duck. Furthermore, your base is only as strong as your ability to defend the door. If a coordinated team breaches your entrance, your turrets won't save you from being overwhelmed in close quarters. You also fall behind in raw combat stats, meaning if someone actually gets inside, you lack the personal skill to fight them off.
A boring, cowardly strategy that wins games by making everyone else quit out of sheer frustration.

XP Speedrun — The Age Grinder

Pure focus on hitting ore and killing animals to hit Age 25, 26, and 27 ASAP. Pet combat is a secondary afterthought.
✓ This is the highest skill-ceiling strategy in the early game. By ignoring combat and purely grinding ore and animals, you unlock the absolute best ranged and melee weapons before anyone else. Once you hit Age 25, you have a massive gear and stat advantage over players who are still stuck fighting in the early Ages. You can literally one-shot early-game players just by having superior weapons. It rewards patience and game knowledge, allowing you to bypass the messy early-game brawls and transition straight into the mid-game dominance phase.
✗ The first ten minutes of this strategy are absolute hell. Because you are ignoring combat and pet synergy, you are incredibly vulnerable to early-game players who are actively looking for fights. You will die repeatedly to Wolf and Bear players because you simply lack the stats to fight back. It requires immense mental fortitude to keep dying, respawn, and keep grinding. If you get tilted and try to fight back before you hit your Age targets, you will completely ruin your own economy and fall behind the curve.
A high-risk, high-reward meta that separates the patient, disciplined grinders from the impatient, easily tilted fighters.

The Rankings

#1 Wolf Aggro Rank 1 of 5
#2 Bear Tank Rank 2 of 5
#3 Rabbit Kite Rank 3 of 5
#4 Base Turtle Rank 4 of 5
#5 XP Speedrun Rank 5 of 5

Here is my definitive ranking of these early-game strategies, from absolute worst to undisputed best. Starting at the bottom, the Bear Tank is the worst. It’s a massive beginner trap; you feel like a god until you realize you’re too slow to hit anything and get kited to death by Age 10. Next is the Base Turtle. It’s incredibly boring and relies entirely on your opponents making mistakes or raging quit. It doesn't actually make you a better player. Moving up, the Wolf Aggro is a solid early-game bully, but it falls off a cliff the second ranged weapons unlock. It’s overrated by players who only play the first ten minutes. In second place, we have the XP Speedrun. This is a brilliant, high-reward strategy, but the early-game vulnerability is just too punishing for most players to handle consistently. Finally, the undisputed best strategy is the Rabbit Kite. Mobility is the true meta in Taming.io. The Rabbit dictates every engagement, scales perfectly into the mid-game, and rewards actual mechanical skill. If you want to win, pick the Rabbit and never stop moving.

Who Should Pick What

If you're an absolute beginner
Go Base Turtle
Building walls and turrets keeps you safe while you learn the controls. It removes the pressure of early-game PvP, letting you focus on understanding Age progression and resource management without getting farmed for XP.
If you love aggression
Pick Wolf Aggro
The fireball skill gives you incredible early-game zone control. You can bully players and melt animals for XP. Just remember to transition to ranged weapons once you hit Age 15, or you will fall off hard.
If you play defensively
Choose Rabbit Kite
Your unmatched speed lets you control the map and dictate every fight. You can grab resources and escape danger instantly. It requires good positioning, but it is the most reliable way to survive and scale into the mid-game.

The Bottom Line

At the end of the day, the Rabbit Kite is the undisputed king of Taming.io's early game. Too many players fall for the trap of picking the Bear because they want to feel tough, or the Wolf because they want to deal damage. But this game is all about positioning and map control. The Rabbit gives you the ultimate tool to survive: the ability to simply walk away from a bad fight. It forces you to play smart, rewards mechanical skill, and scales beautifully into the mid-game. Stop picking the slow tanks, grab the Rabbit, and start controlling the server.

Our Verdict

Taming.io is a surprisingly deep survival sandbox that elevates the standard .io formula with its elemental pet-combat system and intricate base-building mechanics. While the early game feels like a generic resource grinder, the late-game raid defense and strategic pet synergies offer a genuinely rewarding loop for hardcore players. It is an absolute must-play if you love the progression of Ark: Survival Evolved but only have fifteen minutes to spare.

Common Mistakes New Players Make

  1. Ignoring elemental type advantages when building your pet squad, such as bringing Plant pets against a Fire boss. → Always check the elemental triangle and diversify your tamed pets to counter specific raid bosses or enemy types.
  2. Building your base walls directly against resource nodes, leaving no room for defensive structures. → Leave a 2-3 tile gap between your outer walls and resource patches so you can place spike traps and towers to funnel raiders.
  3. Trying to tame adult wild animals or ignoring the success rate mechanics. → Only approach sleeping baby animals and use a slingshot or appletor to significantly boost your taming success rate.
  4. Hoarding gold instead of investing it in the shop for hats and wings that provide passive buffs. → Spend your gold on shop equipment for healing and stat effects, as dying and losing your progress is worse than having the stats to survive.
  5. Failing to assign specific commands to individual pets during base raids or boss fights. → Use the pet command UI to set one pet on defense while sending your high-DPS pets to attack, preventing your entire squad from wandering off.
